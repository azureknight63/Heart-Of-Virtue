COMBAT GLOSSARY

✕
⌕
All Time Moves Resources Position Damage Effects
Beat Time
The unit of combat time. Fights are measured in beats, not turns. On every beat, each move in play advances one step, the enemies act, status effects tick down, and heat drifts back toward neutral.
You'll know a beat has passed when the BEAT counter above the battlefield ticks up. Outside combat, effects count down in steps — one per room you walk into — instead.
The four stages Moves
Every move runs through four stages, each lasting a set number of beats.
Prep — winding up; nothing has happened yet and you can still break off.
Execute — the move lands.
Recoil — the backswing: the hit has landed, but you are still committed and cannot act until it finishes.
Cooldown — you are free to act again, but this move is locked out.
The four-segment bar on a move card is those stages, in that order. Its length compares that move's total commitment against the heaviest move in the list.
Cooldown Moves
The last of a move's four stages. Only that move is unavailable — everything else stays usable, which is why the card says "Available in 5 beats" rather than stopping you from acting. For a standard weapon attack the length is roughly 3 + weapon weight − (endurance ÷ 10) beats: heavier weapons cool slower, and endurance shortens every cooldown you have.
Moves cooling down are stacked in the COOLDOWN tray on the left panel, each showing the beats it still needs. Hover the tray to expand it.
Fatigue (FP) Resources
The orange bar — that which pays for moves. It does not regenerate on its own during a fight.
Endurance raises your maximum fatigue; carrying too much weight lowers it. "Jean recovered 60 FP!" in the log is fatigue points.
Heat Resources
A multiplier on every point of damage Jean deals — shown as 1.62×. Landing hits, parrying, and making enemies miss push it up; missing, being parried, and taking hits pull it down. It drifts 5% back toward 1.00× every beat, so it is a lease, not a bank. It never leaves the range 0.50×–10.00×, and only Jean has one.
The HEAT meter on the left panel names the band you are in and lists exactly what raises and lowers it.
Distance & reach Position
The battlefield is a grid where one square is about one foot, and the "18 ft" under an enemy is the straight-line distance to them. Every move has a minimum and a maximum reach in feet; a target outside it greys the move out with "Enemy out of range (too far)". Advancing or withdrawing covers roughly one to three feet per beat, depending on how much faster you are than what you are chasing.
Moves that reach further than a sword draw their range as a ring around Jean.
Protection & resistance Damage
A blow's power is first scaled by the target's resistance to that kind of damage (1.0 is neutral; lower resists more), then their protection is subtracted as a flat amount, and heat is applied to what is left. Protection appears as "Defense" on the stat panel.
A blow that never gets past protection reads as "struck … but did no damage".
Hit chance Damage
A percentage — your accuracy (a base value plus your finesse and intelligence) minus the target's finesse, adjusted for the situation. If the roll comes in just under your hit chance the blow only grazes and deals half damage; the log calls that "just barely hit".
Target cards preview the hit chance before you commit the beats.
Status effect vs. passive Effects
A status effect is temporary and carries a duration in beats; it ticks down one per beat while you fight (one per step while you explore) and then falls off. A passive is a permanent trait from a skill you have learned — always on, never expires.
Status effects sit in the STATUS column beside the hero; passives in the PASSIVES column.
Breaking off (abort) Moves
While a move is still winding up you can hold the amber control to abandon it. This is not an undo: the beats already spent are gone, and you still pay the move's full cooldown before you can use it again.
The control only exists while a move is in prep — that is the only window in which the move has not yet happened.
? Also opens from any underlined term · Esc to close

Structure. Each entry is name → what it is → how you see it. The second line (amber ▸) is the part that answers the player's actual question — "how do I know a beat has passed" — and it is what tooltips reuse. Terms are grouped, not alphabetised: a player who does not know what a beat is cannot look it up under B. Filter chips narrow the list; the search box filters on term name and body. Scroll lives inside .gl-list only, so the header, search and chips stay pinned. Not modal-blocking: combat is paused for input while the panel is open, so it can safely cover the battlefield.

Confirmed by the maintainer (2026-09-05)

The displayed cooldown number being one larger than the engine's stage length is intended. cooldown_remaining = beats_left + 1 and cooldown_max = stage_beat[3] + 1 (combat_adapter.py:3053–3058), because draining to zero does not itself advance the stage — the next beat does. The glossary states the player-facing number, which is the right one to state; it simply is not the move's declared cooldown stage.

Recoil is a real, intended stage. It represents the time between a hit landing and cooldown starting — the weapon's backswing. The four-stages entry now says so in those terms. Worth noting the UI still never names recoil outside the commitment-bar hover title, so the glossary is currently the only place a player can learn the word.

Fatigue does not regenerate during a fight. The engine never states this — an exhaustive grep finds only Rest, items, a mastery skill and _handle_victory ever adding fatigue, so it was an inferred negative rather than a written rule. Confirmed as intended, and the Fatigue entry now says so outright.

Heat is the name, in the UI and on the wire. The earlier draft called it Momentum in player-facing text while the engine called it heat; that split is gone — everything reads Heat now, so no alias entry is needed.

Still flagged — engine meaning genuinely unclear

1. A beat has no in-fiction duration. Nothing in the engine maps a beat to seconds or to any narrative unit. Every definition above is deliberately mechanical ("the unit of combat time"); if the maintainer wants a fictional gloss ("about one heartbeat"), that is a writing decision, not a fact to be read out of the code.

2. Advance/Withdraw distance is a roll, not a constant. "One to three feet per beat" describes the clamped range of max(1, (rand(0,30) + speed − target.speed) // 10); the typical value depends on the speed gap. Stated as a range on purpose.
