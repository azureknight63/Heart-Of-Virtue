# Scree Strider

A heavy-bodied, flightless bird of Aurelion's eastern slope — the same rocky,
wind-worn ecoregion that produces the Scarp Adder and the Talus Hound. Where
those two are predators, the Scree Strider is a placid, deep-chested forager
built for load-bearing endurance rather than speed. It is the only large
animal that moves easily on both loose switchback scree and wet riverbank
stone, which is the specific reason nomadic caravans on the river route keep
them instead of oxen or mules.

## Appearance

- Flightless, ratite-built bird standing roughly at a tall man's shoulder,
  heavier-boned and more barrel-chested than a sprinting bird would need to be
- Broad, three-toed feet, splayed wide and padded with thick, calloused
  soles — grip loose scree and algae-slick river rock with equal
  reliability, where a hoof or a paw would slide
- Dense double plumage: a close, oiled outer layer that sheds cold mountain
  wind and river spray, over a thick insulating underdown
- A stiff ridge of longer quills running down the spine from neck to tail,
  normally lying flat; raised into a visible fan when startled, threatened,
  or defending territory
- Long, flexible neck and a blunt, powerful beak suited to stripping thorny
  scrub and tearing tough reed growth — forage most livestock won't touch
- Dull, mottled plumage in greys, dust-browns, and faded ochre — camouflage
  that matches the scarp and scrub country rather than open grassland
- Deep chest cavity gives its call a low, resonant, almost subsonic quality
  — audible over running water and wind at a distance no human voice carries

## Habitat & Range

Native to the transitional scarp and scrub country between Grondia's eastern
face and the river valley — the same terrain profiled in
`docs/lore/environments/eastern-approach/eastern-approach-profile.md`. Wild
populations range the switchback slopes and river-adjacent scrub in small,
loose family groups, foraging on vegetation too thorny or too brackish for
other grazers. They are strong swimmers when they choose to be, but generally
prefer to walk river shallows rather than swim them, testing footing with
one splayed foot before committing weight.

They are not found on the open Resolute Plains to the east (the terrain
doesn't suit them) nor deep in the Wailing Badlands to the west (too arid,
too little forage). Their range is specifically the strip of rocky,
river-fed transitional land in between — which is also, not coincidentally,
the strip every east-west caravan has to cross.

## Behavior

- Foraging generalists; the blunt beak and tough digestive tract handle
  scrub, reeds, and river-bottom vegetation other animals reject
- Loose family-group structure in the wild; young imprint hard on whichever
  adult raises them and stay close to that individual for years
- Cautious around unfamiliar people and animals, but not flighty — a Scree
  Strider's instinct under threat is to plant its feet and raise its quill
  ridge, not to bolt. This is precisely the trait that makes them tolerable
  as pack animals: they do not panic and scatter cargo the way a spooked
  mule does
- Territorial about whatever they consider "theirs" — nest sites in the
  wild, cargo and harness gear once domesticated. This shows up in captivity
  as fierce, specific possessiveness over a particular load rather than
  general aggression
- Long memory for individual handlers; a Strider raised by one person from a
  chick will often tolerate no one else touching it for years, sometimes
  never

## Special Traits

- **All-Terrain Footing**: Splayed, padded feet handle loose scree, wet
  rock, and riverbed silt with equal reliability — the trait that makes them
  irreplaceable on the switchback-and-river-crossing route, where hoofed and
  pawed pack animals lose their footing
- **Subsonic Call**: A low, chest-resonant boom that carries over river noise
  and wind — used in the wild to keep a foraging group in contact across
  broken terrain, and by caravans as a location marker when a Strider ranges
  ahead or falls behind on the trail
- **Load Tolerance**: Deep chest and heavy bone structure carry sustained
  loads for a full day's travel at a caravan's walking pace — not a sprinter,
  but tireless over distance
- **Threat Display, Not Flight**: Raises its quill ridge and plants its stance
  rather than bolting when alarmed — stable under load, but genuinely
  dangerous to press if it decides a threat is real
- **Fierce Imprinting**: Bonds hard to a single handler from a young age;
  loyalty this specific is a domestication asset (a caravan's Strider won't
  wander off with a stranger) and a domestication cost (a Strider that loses
  its handler can go off feed and become unmanageable for months)
