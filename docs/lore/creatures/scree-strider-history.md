# The Scree Strider — Domestication History
## How a Wild Slope Bird Became the Backbone of the River Route

This document covers the Scree Strider's place in Aurelion's natural history,
its domestication by nomadic human bands, and what that domestication made
possible for trade and settlement along the eastern river valley. See
`docs/lore/creatures/scree-strider.md` for the species profile (appearance,
behavior, habitat) and `docs/lore/character-profiles/anvil.md` for Anvil, the
individual Scree Strider bonded to Kaelen & Vespera.

---

## Before Domestication

Long before any caravan crossed the river east of Grondia, wild Scree
Striders ranged the scarp and scrub country in loose family groups,
foraging the thorny growth and brackish reed-beds that other animals of the
region left alone. They shared that terrain with the Talus Hound and the
Scarp Adder — predator and ambush hunter respectively — which is very likely
why the species' threat response evolved toward *standing its ground* rather
than fleeing. A Talus Hound pack singles out runners. A Scree Strider that
plants its feet, raises its quill ridge, and meets a hound's charge head-on
with a beak built for tearing tough reed stalks is a much worse bet for the
hound to take.

That same don't-run instinct is the trait that, generations later, made the
species worth the trouble of domesticating.

## The Problem the Species Solved

The river route east of Grondia was never kind to conventional pack animals.
The eastern descent is a switchback trail of loose rock and shifting scree.
The lowland approach beyond it is scrubby and uneven. And the route's
defining obstacle — the river itself, with its shifting current and
silt-bottomed shallows — is exactly the kind of footing that makes a mule
plant its heels and refuse, or a draft ox lose its balance and panic.

Early human traders and nomadic bands working this corridor had two options:
route around the river valley entirely (adding days, and trading the river
crossing for the mountain's southern skirt, which is how most human traders
reach Grondia today — see `docs/lore/environments/eastern-approach/eastern-approach-profile.md`),
or find an animal that could actually cross where the river crossing was.

Nomadic bands who worked the river route directly noticed what Golemite
scouts had likely known for longer: the local Scree Striders crossed the
same river daily without apparent difficulty, testing the footing with one
splayed foot before committing weight, and never lost their nerve on the
switchbacks the way a horse or mule would. A wild animal that already solved
your logistics problem is a domestication candidate. The rest was time and
patience.

## Domestication

The process was slow, generational, and specific to river-route nomads —
it never spread to the wider settled world the way ox and mule husbandry did,
because nowhere else needed it. Domestication centered on the species'
hardest trait to work with and its greatest asset at once: fierce, specific
imprinting.

A wild-caught adult Scree Strider is nearly impossible to work with — it
recognizes no one as its handler and treats every load as something to
defend rather than carry. A chick raised by hand from its first days,
however, imprints on its raiser the way a duckling imprints on whatever it
sees first, and stays loyally, sometimes exclusively, bonded to that person
for the rest of its working life. Nomadic bands learned to take chicks
early, raise them within a single family or partnership, and accept that the
resulting animal would work for that handler and largely tolerate no one
else. This is not a training failure to be corrected — it is the domestication
model working as intended. A caravan doesn't want a pack animal that will
calmly load up for a stranger. It wants one that won't.

The tradeoff is real and every nomad who works with Striders knows it: lose
your handler, and the animal you're left with may refuse to feed, refuse to
carry, and take months — sometimes longer — to accept a new bond, if it ever
fully does. Nomad households that keep Scree Striders plan for this the way
they plan for any other structural risk of the road.

## What It Made Possible

Once river-route nomads had pack animals that could actually manage the
switchback-and-crossing route on a working caravan's timeline, the route
itself became viable as a real trade corridor rather than a shortcut only
foot-travelers used. That, in turn, is a large part of why a seasonal camp
exists at all on the river's east bank: a mobile trading community — like
the one Kaelen and Vespera belong to — can only tear down, relocate, and
rebuild along the river route several times a year *because* the animals
hauling their forge stock, armor racks, and canvas can actually make the
crossing without losing a season's stock to the water.

This is a small piece of a larger pattern in Aurelion's history: trade
corridors follow whatever solves the terrain, not the other way around. The
Golemites of Grondia built GateEast for scouting and resource traffic, not
trade — the citadel's real commerce arrives via the southern skirt
waystation, the route most human traders still use
(see `docs/lore/environments/world.md`, "Southeast — The Plains Approach and
the Trader Waystation"). The river route stayed a nomad-only corridor for
exactly as long as it stayed impassable to ordinary pack animals. Scree
Strider husbandry is the reason it didn't stay impassable — and the reason
Jean meets a working forge and armor stall at a river crossing that, on a
map, has no obvious business supporting one.

## Lore Integration Notes

**Timeline**: Domestication is old — multiple human generations, predating
Kaelen and Vespera's own itinerant trading life by a wide margin. It is
established practice among river-route nomads, not a recent innovation.

**Connection to the wider setting**: Ties the Nomad Camp's material culture
(the reason Iron & Oath can exist as a seasonal, mobile stall at all) to the
regional fauna already established as native to this exact terrain (Scarp
Adder, Talus Hound). Reinforces that this stretch of Aurelion has its own
coherent, load-bearing ecology, not just a backdrop for Jean's transit
between Chapter 2 and Chapter 3.

**Narrative function**: Available as background color if Jean asks Kaelen,
Vespera, or any nomad NPC about Anvil, the river crossing, or how the camp
manages to relocate at all. Not required reading for the Chapter 2→3
transition — it exists to reward a player who is curious about the world
rather than to gate any story beat.
